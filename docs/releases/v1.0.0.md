The first downloadable release of Protect Ears, an experimental macOS volume guard for AirPods Max.

**We do not know whether Protect Ears will protect your hearing. It has not been proven to prevent hearing damage. Do not rely on it as hearing protection.** The app can fail or react too late, and loud audio can still reach your headphones. A volume percentage is not a measured sound-pressure or safety limit. Read the [full hearing-safety disclaimer](https://github.com/turnertop/protect-ears/blob/v1.0.0/DISCLAIMER.md).

## Download and install

- **Protect-Ears-macOS-universal.dmg**: open it, drag Protect Ears into Applications, eject the disk image, then open the app from Applications.
- **Protect-Ears-macOS-universal.zip**: ZIP alternative; extract it and move the app into Applications.
- **SHA256SUMS.txt**: SHA-256 checksums for both downloads.

Requires **macOS 13 or later**. Both downloads contain the same **universal app for Apple silicon and Intel Macs**. No Xcode or Command Line Tools are needed. The source-code archives are for developers; choose the DMG or ZIP asset above for the app.

**Signing notice:** this release is ad-hoc signed. It is not signed with an Apple Developer ID and has not been notarized by Apple, so macOS may block it on first open. Only if you trust this download, follow [Apple's instructions for allowing this individual app](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102445). Do not disable Gatekeeper or remove quarantine attributes.

## What it does

- Attempts to keep selected headphone output volume at four steps (25%) or five steps (31.25%); four steps is the default.
- Automatically selects AirPods Max and remembers their device identity after detection.
- Watches connected selected outputs, volume and route changes, reconnections, and wake, with a periodic fallback check.
- Attempts to mute a selected device if a cap cannot be verified, and displays a warning.
- Offers optional Start at login. Enable it after moving the app to Applications.
- Stores preferences locally, records no audio, sends no data, and requires no Accessibility or microphone permission.

The app only acts while running. It reacts after volume changes and is **not a strict audio-path limiter, medical device, or certified hearing-protection device**. The name does not imply proven hearing protection.
